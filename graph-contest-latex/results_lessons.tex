\begin{frame}{Lessons Learned}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The starting point beats the search.} Every order-of-magnitude win came
          from a better initial layout; no modification of the annealing walk survived a
          paired A/B.
    \item \textbf{Measure before optimising.} The two biggest engineering wins
          (restart cost, repair) were found by profiling, not intuition.
    \item \textbf{Single-seed comparisons are noise.} Wall-clock budgets make even
          same-seed reruns diverge under different machine load; we moved to paired,
          equal-load, multi-seed A/Bs for every decision.
    \item \textbf{Negative results are results.} Five rejected ideas, each with a
          documented experiment, protect the project from re-trying them later.
    \item \textbf{Infrastructure compounds.} Best-ever tracking, the live dashboard and
          one-command A/Bs made the decisive experiments cheap.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What Remains \& Takeaway}
  \textbf{Open front: Automatic-6} (dense, 200 nodes / 3\,000 edges).
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Resists every init (est.\ 473 crossings/edge even after a stress layout ---
          stress init actually \emph{hurts} here: 765 vs.\ plain SA's 624);
          $k$ scales with budget: $632 \to 624$ by going to $15$\,min $\times$ 8 workers.
    \item Plan: 30--60\,min runs to close the gap to 621 (other teams) / 568 (winner @49\,min).
  \end{itemize}
